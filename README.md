# biodegradable-nanocomposite-hydrogel
Synthesis and characterization of a biodegradable nanocomposite hydrogel based on corn starch and salep modified with Matricaria chamomilla.
Biodegradable Nanocomposite Hydrogel

Project Overview

This project focuses on the synthesis and characterization of a biodegradable nanocomposite hydrogel based on corn starch and salep, modified with Matricaria chamomilla, for potential application as an absorbent material in disposable diapers.

Research Objective

The main objective was to develop a biodegradable, water-absorbent material using renewable natural polymers and a natural bioactive compound as an alternative approach to conventional petroleum-based absorbent materials.

Materials

- Corn starch
- Salep
- Matricaria chamomilla
- Acrylamide
- Ammonium persulfate (APS)
- N,N′-Methylene bisacrylamide
- Distilled water

Synthesis

A semi-interpenetrating polymer network (semi-IPN) hydrogel was prepared using corn starch and salep as natural polymeric components, followed by chemical crosslinking and incorporation of Matricaria chamomilla.

Different concentrations of chamomile were investigated to evaluate their influence on the structural, morphological, thermal, swelling, and antibacterial properties of the hydrogel.

Characterization

The synthesized materials were investigated using several analytical techniques:

- FTIR spectroscopy
- X-ray diffraction (XRD)
- Field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM)
- Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX/EDS)
- Atomic force microscopy (AFM)
- Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)
- Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) analysis
- Swelling and saline absorption studies
- Sol/gel fraction analysis
- Antibacterial evaluation

Research Focus

The project combines concepts from:

- Organic Chemistry
- Green and Sustainable Chemistry
- Biodegradable Polymers
- Hydrogel Materials
- Nanocomposite Materials
- Natural Products
- Polymer Modification and Crosslinking

Potential Application

The developed biodegradable hydrogel was investigated as a potential sustainable absorbent material for disposable diaper applications.
